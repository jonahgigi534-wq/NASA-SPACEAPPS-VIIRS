Two-minute tour for judges

Each line of the challenge brief, and the one click that shows it. Every link opens the live app in a prepared view; the address bar keeps the view, so any screen can be shared the same way (Copy link next to the area name).

The brief asks forWhere to look
Harmonize MODIS and VIIRS so "the sensor of origin no longer matters" Raw VIIRS view: the long-term chart at the bottom jumps in 2012. Switch Satellite record to Harmonized: the jump is gone. Method and test results: How it works, More checks.
Select a region: draw on a map, or enter latitude and longitude The app: draw a rectangle, polygon or circle on the map, use Type coordinates instead (box or point + radius), search a place name, or pick a big fire event.
A specific date range Dates and settings: date pickers and quick ranges. "Normal" years are adjustable too.
A burning calendar: number of hotspots from the early 2000s to the present Burning calendar: every month since 2001; by week; click any square for its days. Grey = no satellite data (never shown as zero), hatched = provisional.
Which periods of the year see more fires Patterns & critical periods: typical month by month with the fire season shaded, highest-risk months and weeks, and how often each month has been unusually busy.
Which years had more burning activity Same tab: yearly totals with the busiest three highlighted, and the long-term trend (Theil–Sen slope + Mann–Kendall test).
How a current fire compares with previous ones Fires vs past: hotspots grouped into fire clusters, the latest ranked against every cluster since 2001. Here the largest is the Hermits Peak / Calf Canyon fire of 2022. Show puts any fire on the map.
The current month against the historical median Now vs history: this month so far vs the median for the same days, its rank, and each day vs normal. Live FIRMS data, refreshed hourly. Unusual right now (left panel) lists the squares furthest above normal this month.
Anomalies against multi-year averages Calendar "vs normal" and Fire on the map → vs normal; the summary gives % of median, z-scores are in the analytics CSV.
Number of fires detected (key figures) The strip above the summary: hotspots in range, vs normal, busiest month and year, fire season. Measure switches to fire power (MW).
Accessible color ramps that show similarities and differences across months Viridis (counts) and purple–orange (vs normal), both colorblind-safe; legends with units; keyboard navigation; data tables for screen readers. Automated accessibility audit (axe-core): no serious issues.
Download analytics and visualizations Download panel: daily analytics CSV, a one-page printable briefing; the ⤓ button on every chart saves a PNG.
A CSV of the hotspots detected during a search Hotspots in this area (CSV), also GeoJSON and KML for GIS and Google Earth.
For early warning, emergency responders, scientists, land managers, firefighters A plain-language summary for every search, the "unusual right now" list, shareable links for handing a view to a colleague, and a phone layout.

Data

NASA FIRMS active fires: MODIS Collection 6.1 (Terra, Aqua) and VIIRS 375 m (S-NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21), plus MODIS land cover (MCD12Q1). DOIs and limitations on How it works.
