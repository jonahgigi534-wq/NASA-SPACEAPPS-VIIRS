## Project name
FireBridge

## Short summary (one or two sentences)
FireBridge joins NASA's MODIS and VIIRS fire satellites into one consistent record of fire
activity from 2001 to today, and turns it into a burning activity calendar for any area you
draw on a map: fire seasons, unusual years, this month versus the historical median, and a CSV
of every hotspot.

## Project details / how it meets the challenge
Satellites have tracked active-fire hotspots for more than 20 years, but the record is split
between MODIS (1 km pixels, 2000 onward, with data collection planned to end in 2027) and VIIRS
(375 m pixels, from 2012), which reports far more hotspots for the same fires: on our test years,
raw VIIRS counts are +659.69% higher than MODIS Aqua. Put end to end, the two records show a fake
explosion of fire in 2012.

**Harmonization.** We translate VIIRS hotspot counts into MODIS-equivalent counts using quantile
mapping, learned from 2012–2018 when MODIS Aqua and VIIRS S-NPP flew nearly the same orbit,
separately for each region, land-cover type and day/night, then scaled to the full Terra + Aqua
record. On years the method never saw (2019–2026) its typical monthly error is 971 hotspots per
month against 1,212 for one global ratio, with a bias of −0.88% (raw VIIRS: +659.69%). On NOAA-20,
a satellite never used in fitting, the error is 882 against 1,206. We report where it struggles
(the Amazon, slightly high in Texas) and why.

**The web application.** Users draw a rectangle, polygon or circle, or type coordinates, and
pick dates. They get key figures and a plain-language summary; a month-by-year burning
calendar from 2001 to today with accessible, colorblind-safe color ramps (counts or anomaly vs
normal); the months that see the most fire, the fire season and high-risk months; the busiest
years; and the current month compared with the median for the same days, using live FIRMS data.
Missing satellite data is shown as missing, never as zero fire. Everything can be downloaded:
the hotspots in the search as CSV, the analytics table as CSV, and every chart as PNG. A toggle
shows the raw MODIS and raw VIIRS records, so anyone can see the 2012 jump disappear.

**Who it's for.** Early-warning and emergency responders, firefighters, land managers and
scientists who need to know quickly whether this season is unusual, when fire is most likely,
and how today compares with the past 25 years.

## NASA data used
- NASA FIRMS MODIS Collection 6.1 active fires (Terra, Aqua): MCD14ML archive
  (doi:10.5067/FIRMS/MODIS/MCD14ML) and MCD14DL near-real-time
  (doi:10.5067/FIRMS/MODIS/MCD14DL.NRT.0061)
- NASA FIRMS VIIRS 375 m active fires, Collection 2: S-NPP (doi:10.5067/FIRMS/VIIRS/VNP14IMGT_NRT.002),
  NOAA-20 (doi:10.5067/FIRMS/VIIRS/VJ114IMGT_NRT.002), NOAA-21 (doi:10.5067/VIIRS/VJ214IMG_NRT.002),
  plus the FIRMS area API for the latest days
- MODIS Land Cover Type MCD12Q1 v061 via NASA AppEEARS (doi:10.5067/MODIS/MCD12Q1.061)

The data shaped the project: the size of the MODIS-to-VIIRS jump made a correction necessary,
the 2012–2018 overlap made it learnable, the land-cover data showed one ratio doesn't fit every
landscape, and FIRMS missing-data days led us to treat gaps as unknown rather than zero.

website, tests, the deploy and video scripts, and drafts of the documentation. The team
reviewed and ran everything. All numbers come from the pipeline's outputs on NASA data; no
images or video were generated by AI. Details: docs/AI_USE.md in the repository.
